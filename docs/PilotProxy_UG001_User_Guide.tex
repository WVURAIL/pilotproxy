\section{Introduction and Scope}

PilotProxy detects ATSC 1.0 digital television (DTV) in the CHIME and CHORD radio telescopes. An 8-VSB DTV channel is 6 MHz wide and carries a steady pilot tone 309.441 kHz above its lower edge. The detector looks for a small excess of power at the pilot over nearby fine frequency bins. It gives one statistic for each block of 41.94304 ms, and the block is rejected when the excess passes a threshold word.

The detector runs inside Kotekan as the \code{cudaPilotProxyDetector} command of a \code{cudaProcess} stage. The \code{DtvRfiMask} stage folds the decisions into the RFI mask that the correlator reads. Under fine decision v3, \code{DtvRfiMask} makes these decisions itself, from the statistic that the detector publishes. Both stages read their numbers from a \emph{runtime bundle}, a directory of five files made by the PilotProxy Python tools.

This guide is for the person who runs the detector. It covers:
\begin{itemize}
  \item building PilotProxy and the Kotekan detector;
  \item the layout and content of a runtime bundle, file by file;
  \item the record, calibrate, test and deploy loop that makes a bundle;
  \item the Kotekan configuration for the CHORD Pathfinder and the CHIME upgrade;
  \item checking a run, and the common failures.
\end{itemize}

The guide does not explain why the detector is built the way it is. The design reasoning is in the PilotProxy-DD001 Design Document (\code{docs/PilotProxy_DD001_Design_Document.tex}). Other references are listed in Table~\ref{tab:references}.

\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:definitions}

\subsection{Signal Path}

For each bound channel, the detector packs one block of voltages from every input into 128 windows of \(K\) samples. \(K=128\) on CHIME (16384-sample blocks) and \(K=64\) on the Pathfinder (8192-sample blocks). Three weight terms are applied to each window: the \emph{target} term at the pilot and the \emph{lower} and \emph{upper} reference terms two detector bins away on each side. A fixed-point FFT along the window axis then gives \ppFineNumBins\ \emph{fine bins} of \ppFineBinHz\ Hz in each term, and the powers are summed over the inputs. The result is the exact \code{uint64} fine powers \(S_t[b]\), \(S_l[b]\) and \(S_u[b]\) of the block, for \(b=0,\dots,255\).

The \emph{fine ratio} of bin \(b\) is
\begin{equation}
  F_2[b]=\frac{2\,S_t[b]}{S_l[b]+S_u[b]}.
\end{equation}
A bin with \(S_l[b]+S_u[b]=0\) has no ratio, and in version 1 of the test it cannot fire.

\subsection{Decision}

The current test is fine decision v3, \code{fine_decision_v3} (kernel core 4.2.0). The kernel makes no decision. For each block and bound channel it publishes the pair \([n_{\mathrm{peak}},d_\rho]\):
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{peak}}=\max_{0\le b<256} 2\,S_t[b],
  \qquad
  d_\rho=\text{rank 63 of } \{S_l[b]+S_u[b] : b=0,2,\dots,254\}.
\end{equation}
\(n_{\mathrm{peak}}\) is the largest target power over all 256 fine bins. There is no anchor, half-width or search window. The odd bins of the zero-padded transform lie between the independent even bins, so a pilot between two independent bins still sets the peak. \(d_\rho\) is the value of zero-based rank 63, in ascending order, of the reference power over the 128 even fine bins, zeros included: the median reference. The median ignores lines and stray bins while fewer than half of the even bins carry them, so no bin is excluded (Figure~\ref{fig:finebins}). A v3 profile therefore carries only its words.

The consumer decides. With its word \(W\), it keeps the block when
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{peak}}\cdot 2^{16} \;\le\; W\,d_\rho ,
\end{equation}
with both products formed exactly in 128 bits, and flags it otherwise. The threshold \(\eta=W/2^{16}\) is the word on a Q16 grid. When \(d_\rho=0\), at least half the even bins have zero reference power (lost packets, or every input flagged), and the block has no decision: consumers reject it. A row the detector did not test is \((0,2^{64}-1)\), and every word keeps it. The test is \code{pilotproxy_v3_keep(n_peak, d_rho, word)} in \code{cuda/pilotproxy_decision.h}. It returns 1 to keep, 0 to flag, and \(-1\) when \(d_\rho=0\). Its Python counterpart is \code{fine_decision.fine_keep_v3}, which returns \code{None} in place of \(-1\) and raises on a word outside \([1,2^{64}-1]\). The GPU entry point is \code{PilotProxy_Compute_FineStatV3_U64}, and the Python reference of the pair is \code{fine_decision.fine_stat_v3}. The keeping word of a block is the smallest word that keeps it (\code{fine_keep_word_v3}).

A v3 profile carries the default word \code{cfar_multiplier_q16}, for any backend with no word of its own, and optionally the power-limit (BAO) word \code{power_limit_multiplier_q16}, which N2 applies where calibration set one (Section~\ref{sec:threshold}). Kotekan's \code{DtvRfiMask} applies \code{power_limit_multiplier_q16} if present, and \code{cfar_multiplier_q16} otherwise, to the pair in \code{dtv_stat}. This needs kotekan\#1924, which is not yet merged into \code{develop}. Kotekan \code{develop} accepts \code{fine_decision_v1} and \code{fine_decision_v2} bundles (since \#1914), and \code{calibrate-bundle} writes v2 by default until Kotekan runs v3 (Section~\ref{sec:calibrate}).

\subsubsection{Versions 1 and 2}

Older bundles, and Kotekan \code{develop} until kotekan\#1924 merges, use versions 1 and 2. These decide in the kernel and write the mask. Their profile gives each channel an \emph{anchor bin} \(a\), a half-width \(w\), a set of \emph{bulk bins} \(\mathcal{B}\) and a zero-based \emph{rank} \(r\) beside the word \(W\). The pilot is searched for in the \emph{designated set}, the \(2w+1\) bins \(a-w,\dots,a+w\) taken modulo 256, and the bulk is every second bin outside the designated set and its guards. Version 1, \code{fine_decision_v1}, rejects the block when \(\max_{b\in a\pm w}F_2[b]>(W/2^{16})\,R\), where \(R\) is the fine ratio of rank \(r\) among the bulk bins, in ascending order. Version 2, \code{fine_decision_v2}, reads the target alone. It rejects the block when \(\max_{b\in a\pm w}2\,S_t[b]>(W/2^{16})\,D\), where \(D\) is the reference power \(S_l[b]+S_u[b]\) of rank \(r\) among the bulk bins with nonzero reference power. Both use exact integer cross products. When the bulk has \(r\) or fewer usable bins, the test is invalid: the mask bit is 0 and the fine support row is \((0,n)\). Calibration used \(w=32\) (65 designated bins) and the bulk median as the rank. The version 2 kernel entry points carry \code{V2} in their names.

\section{Install and Build}

\subsection{PilotProxy}

PilotProxy is installed from a checkout, in a virtual environment, with the extras each task needs:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
git clone https://github.com/WVURAIL/pilotproxy.git
cd pilotproxy
python -m pip install -e ".[test]"          # tests, h5py, matplotlib
python -m pip install -e ".[n2,plot]"       # read dtv_fine_powers files, plot spectrograms
pilot-proxy --help
\end{lstlisting}
The \code{n2} extra installs \code{h5py} and \code{hdf5plugin}, which are needed to read the \code{dtv_fine_powers} files of a run. The \code{plot} extra installs Matplotlib for \code{run-spectrogram} and \code{process-run}. The \code{cuda} extra installs \code{cupy-cuda12x} for the GPU paths of the standalone testbench and the archive scan.

The bundle commands (\code{export-runtime-weight-bundle}, \code{validate-runtime-weight-bundle}, \code{calibrate-bundle}, \code{test-bundle}, \code{qualify-bundle}, \code{plan-run}, \code{process-run}, \code{run-spectrogram} and \code{run-scores}) compute the fine powers and words with the Python integer path. They need no GPU. A day of fine powers of one channel takes about 13 GB of memory during calibration, because one channel is held at a time.

\subsection{CUDA Kernel}

The CUDA library is needed only for the standalone testbench and the archive scan. On a GPU host:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
make build-kernel   # SM detected from nvidia-smi; SM=<arch> overrides it
\end{lstlisting}
This builds \code{cuda/libpilotproxy.so} and stages a copy at \code{~/.cache/pilot_proxy/libpilotproxy.so}. The CUDA toolchain is described in \code{docs/USAGE.md}.

\subsection{Tests}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
make test-python    # Python tests, no GPU
make test-kernel    # CUDA and kernel tests, needs a GPU and nvcc
make release-check  # adds the CPU C/C++ reference checks and bundle validation
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Kotekan}

Kotekan carries a copy of the detector core in \code{external/pilotproxy}, pinned by \code{VENDOR.json}. The detector is built with \code{USE_CUDA=ON}. The stage checks bundle digests with OpenSSL's SHA-256, so Kotekan must also be built with OpenSSL. Without it the stage stops at startup with \code{Kotekan was built without OpenSSL}. The Kotekan tests of the detector are run with:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
bash tests/ci-scripts/pilotproxy/run_pilotproxy_tests.sh "$PWD/build"
\end{lstlisting}
This script checks the vendored files against \code{VENDOR.json}, installs the matching PilotProxy commit, exports a CHORD and a CHIME bundle, and runs the integration tests. These tests check the code. \code{test-bundle} (Section~\ref{sec:test}) checks the numbers in a bundle. Both are needed after a change to the detector core.

\section{Quickstart}

These steps check a shipped bundle and process a recorded Pathfinder run. No GPU is needed.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Install PilotProxy with \code{.[n2,plot]} as above.
  \item Validate a bundle. Kotekan installs its bundles under \code{<prefix>/etc/kotekan/}:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
B=/usr/local/etc/kotekan/chord/dtv_bundles/pathfinder-commissioning-e2e90219
(cd "$B" && sha256sum -c sha256sums.txt)
pilot-proxy validate-runtime-weight-bundle --bundle-dir "$B"
\end{lstlisting}
  \item Record a detect-only run with \code{dtv_calibration_mode: true} and recording enabled (Section~\ref{sec:pathfinder}).
  \item Split, calibrate, test, plot and summarize the run in one step:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy process-run --bundle-dir "$B" \
  --run "/mnt/cs00/data/dtv/acq_*/dtv_fine_powers*.h5" \
  --epoch "2026-11 Pathfinder commissioning" --calibration-share 0.5 \
  --output-dir run1
\end{lstlisting}
  \item Read \code{run1/summary.md} and the spectrograms under \code{run1/spectrograms/}. If the new bundle in \code{run1/bundle/} is to be deployed, add it to Kotekan by a pull request (Section~\ref{sec:deploy}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Runtime Bundles}
\label{sec:bundles}

\subsection{Layout}

A runtime bundle is one directory with five files (Table~\ref{tab:bundlefiles}). Kotekan reads two of them directly: \code{pilot_profiles.json} and \code{weights.bin}, through \code{pilot_profiles_path} and \code{weights_path}. It reads the other three from the same directory to check the digests. A bundle is never edited in place. \code{calibrate-bundle} refuses an existing output directory, and a new calibration gets a new directory.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Files of a runtime bundle.}
\label{tab:bundlefiles}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{|L{0.22\linewidth}|Y|L{0.17\linewidth}|L{0.20\linewidth}|}
\hline
\textbf{File} & \textbf{Holds} & \textbf{Written by} & \textbf{Read by}\\ \hline
\code{pilot_profiles.json} & One profile per channel: receiver binding, weight offset, and the fine calibration (the words, and in v1 and v2 the anchor, bulk and rank). & Export; rewritten by \code{calibrate-bundle} & Kotekan detector; all PilotProxy run tools\\ \hline
\code{weights.bin} & The weight profiles of all channels, concatenated in profile order. & Export & Kotekan detector; calibration\\ \hline
\pfield{weights.manifest.json} & Weight format, geometry and the placement of each channel's target and reference cells. & Export & Validator; Kotekan reads \code{weights_sha256}\\ \hline
\pfield{detector_contract.json} & The receiver-neutral arithmetic contract: window size, terms, bit widths, ratio definitions. & Export & Validator; \code{test-bundle}\\ \hline
\code{sha256sums.txt} & The SHA-256 of the other four files. & Export and \code{calibrate-bundle} & Kotekan; validator; \code{sha256sum -c}\\ \hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

``Export'' means \code{export-runtime-weight-bundle}, which \code{calibrate-bundle} also calls. An exported bundle has every profile at status \code{pending_campaign}. \code{calibrate-bundle} then fills the fine calibration of each profile, rewrites \code{pilot_profiles.json}, and writes \code{sha256sums.txt} again.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{weights.bin}}{weights.bin}}

\code{weights.bin} is the binary weight bank. It is the concatenation of one \code{int8} profile of shape \([3,K]\) per channel, in the order of \code{profiles}: the target term, then the lower reference, then the upper reference. The file has no header. Its size is the number of profiles times \(3K\) bytes. The shipped CHIME bundle \code{chime-first-56005bb7} has 24 profiles of 384 bytes, 9216 bytes in all. Kotekan checks that the profile offsets account for every byte of the file.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{sha256sums.txt}}{sha256sums.txt} and the Digests}
\label{sec:checksums}

\code{sha256sums.txt} has one line per file, in the format of \code{sha256sum}: the hex digest, two spaces, and the file name. It lists \code{detector_contract.json}, \code{pilot_profiles.json}, \code{weights.bin} and \code{weights.manifest.json}. A bundle is checked by hand with:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
cd <bundle> && sha256sum -c sha256sums.txt
pilot-proxy validate-runtime-weight-bundle --bundle-dir <bundle>
\end{lstlisting}

Three kinds of digest appear in a bundle (Figure~\ref{fig:bundle_digests}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{File digests.} \code{sha256sums.txt} holds the SHA-256 of the bytes of each of the four files. Any edit to a file breaks its line.
  \item \textbf{Weight digest.} \code{weights_sha256} in \code{pilot_profiles.json} and in \code{weights.manifest.json} is the SHA-256 of \code{weights.bin}. It ties the profiles to the bank.
  \item \textbf{Contract digest.} \code{detector_contract_sha256} is the SHA-256 of the contract serialized with sorted keys and no spaces (separators \code{,} and \code{:}). It is therefore not the digest of the file's bytes in \code{sha256sums.txt}. For \code{chime-first-56005bb7}, the file digest begins \code{650139c9} and the contract digest begins \code{db5badce}.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Loop: Record, Calibrate, Test, Deploy}
\label{sec:loop}

The same loop makes a first bundle, a bundle for a new telescope, and a recalibration (Figure~\ref{fig:loop}). A detect-only run of the node records the fine powers, \code{calibrate-bundle} sets the default word and the BAO word from them, and the next bundle carries both words back to the node. The words stay fixed during a run, and each new bundle is tested before it is deployed.

\subsection{Step 1: Record}
\label{sec:record}

Calibration reads the captures of each channel to be bound and of the control channel, channel 37. Three capture formats are accepted, named in the plan by \code{capture_format} (Table~\ref{tab:formats}).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Capture formats.}

\hline

The best source is the detector itself, since a detect-only run gives far more captures than baseband dumps. A merged Pathfinder baseband file is chunked over all frequencies, so a few channels are first copied to a small file:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
python3 tools/extract_baseband_channels.py --input baseband_subset.00000000.h5 \
  --output day1.h5 --coarse-freq 2531 2715 2869 3115 --blocks 20
\end{lstlisting}

The default word needs enough control frames: calibration stops when the independent null scores times \(1-\)\code{null_quantile} is below 1. Under v3 each control frame gives one score, so the 0.99 quantile needs at least 100 control frames. The BAO test needs at least 30 captures of a channel (\code{min_captures}), as does a v1 or v2 knee (\code{min_knee_captures}). Calibration and test data are taken at different times. A detect-only recording of several days calibrates on its first part and tests on the days after. \code{tools/calibration_length.py} finds the shortest first part whose words still pass on the rest, and it is at least a day, since conditions change within one. Every deployed word comes from such a recording on the telescope where it is used. The archive values characterize the method and are not deployed.

A telescope with no data starts from its parameters. A plan with empty capture lists, \code{"control": null} and \code{"num_inputs"} gives a first bundle whose control word comes from simulated white noise for that number of inputs. This bundle is enough to record the data for the next one. \code{chime-first-56005bb7} was made this way with 2048 inputs.

\subsection{Step 2: Calibrate}
\label{sec:calibrate}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy calibrate-bundle --plan calibrate.json --output-dir bundle
\end{lstlisting}
For each channel, \code{calibrate-bundle} exports the weights and computes the fine powers of every block with the deployed integer path. Under v3 it finds the control word from the control, scales it to the channel for the default word, and, with the power-limit threshold, runs the BAO test for the BAO word. Under v1 and v2 it finds the anchor, sets the bulk and rank, finds the floor from the control and the knee, and picks the word. It then writes \code{bundle/}, validates it, and writes \code{bundle.report.json}. A bundle that fails validation is removed.

\subsubsection{Anchor}
\label{sec:anchor}

The anchor starts at the nominal fine bin of the pilot. Calibration looks within 30 bins of it for the bin with the largest 0.9 quantile of the fine ratio. That bin becomes the anchor (\code{anchor_source: measured}) when the blocks above the floor are too many to come from the null. Otherwise the nominal bin is kept (\code{nominal}), and a plan \code{anchors} entry gives \code{plan}. A channel marked \code{nominal} showed no pilot in its captures, and a capture with the pilot on should replace it. Under v1 and v2 the anchor places the search. Under v3 the search takes every bin, and the anchor search only decides whether the pilot was seen: a channel marked \code{nominal} gets no BAO word (\code{pilot not seen}), and a plan \code{anchors} entry overrides that gate.

\subsubsection{Word}
\label{sec:word}

Under v3, the report gives \code{word_source} \code{control word x scale} for the default word: the control's word at \code{null_quantile}, from the exact keep words of its frames, times the channel scale \(\lambda_c = \mu_{0,c}\,g_c/(\mu_{0,37}\,g_{37})\), rounded up to Q16. \(\mu_0\) is the null level of the stored weights, and \(g\) the passband factor of the plan's \code{default_word} block. A word from \code{words} or \code{word_q16} gives \code{plan}. The default word does not depend on the channel's own pilot, so a channel with a pilot in nearly every frame is masked whenever the pilot is seen. A frame mask recovers little of such a channel.

The BAO word is kept apart, in \code{power_limit_word_q16}, with \code{power_limit_word_source} \code{power limit}, or \code{none, power limit: <reason>} for a channel with no passing word (Section~\ref{sec:threshold}).

Under v1 and v2, \code{word_source} is one of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{knee}: the pilot was seen, the channel had at least 30 captures, the pilot share was at most 0.9, and the knee lay above the floor;
  \item \code{floor, no pilot}, \code{floor, <n> captures of 30}, \code{floor, pilot in nearly every frame}, \code{floor, knee below it}, \code{floor, no captures}: the floor, for the stated reason;
  \item \code{plan}: from \code{words} or \code{word_q16};
  \item \code{power limit}, or \code{floor, power limit: <reason>}: Section~\ref{sec:threshold}.
\end{itemize}
Every capture has equal total weight in the knee, so a long capture does not set the curve.

\subsection{Choosing the Threshold}
\label{sec:threshold}

A plan takes one of two thresholds.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{"threshold": "power_limit"}, the BAO test, gives each channel the BAO word: the word that keeps the most blocks while the upper residual shelf power meets a checked limit. It needs a recorded control and takes no \code{words} or \code{word_q16}. Under v3 the default word is written beside it.
  \item \code{"threshold": "knee"} (default) gives each channel only the default word of the previous section.
\end{itemize}

The power-limit plan names its inputs:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
{
  "threshold": "power_limit",
  "power_limit": {
    "table": "channel_table.json",
    "limits": "limits.json",
    "coupling": {"29": 0.2},
    "curvature": {"29": [1.0e-6, 1.0e-7]},
    "margin": 1.0e-6
  }
}
\end{lstlisting}
\code{table} is the RFIsher channel table of this telescope and \code{limits} maps each channel to its checked power limit in mK\(^2\). Both are required. \code{coupling} is the fraction of the kept shelf power that reaches the map, 1 by default. \code{curvature} gives each calibrated channel the passband offset of the ratio at its pilot less that at the control position, with its standard error, and \code{margin} is added to the upper estimate. Both are required, in units of the shelf estimate, and a term measured as none is written as 0. Leaving them out can read a channel low, toward a pass: the offset difference is negative on some channels, and where an estimate clips at zero only the error terms remain. For CHIME they are the dissertation's offset difference and route term (\code{estimate_offset.json}, fields \code{curvature}, \code{curvature_se} and \code{route_disagreement}). \code{min_captures} defaults to 30. Under v3, a channel with a passing word carries it as \code{power_limit_multiplier_q16} beside its default word, and the test reads the control at \(\lfloor u/\lambda_c\rfloor\), the channel word \(u\) mapped onto the control by the scale of the default word. The excess is the frame-level ratio \(F/\mu_0-1\), each channel and the control at the null level \(\mu_0\) of its own weights; the report gives \code{estimate} and \code{mu0}. A channel with no passing word carries only its default word, and the operating policy masks it whole. Under v1 and v2, the passing word is the channel's \code{cfar_multiplier_q16} (\code{word_source} \code{power limit}), and a channel with no passing word holds the floor. The outcomes go to \code{<dir>.decisions.json}, which \code{compile-operating-policy} reads (\code{docs/CHANNEL_OPERATING_POLICY.md}). This threshold holds the control in memory beside each channel. The meaning of the test is described in the Design Document.

\code{plan-run} and \code{process-run} take \code{--threshold power_limit --power-limit inputs.json}, where the file holds the \code{power_limit} object, and \code{--decision-version fine_decision_v3} for a v3 bundle. Without it they write v2, which Kotekan \code{develop} runs until kotekan\#1924 merges. For \code{calibrate-bundle}, these are the plan keys \code{threshold}, \code{power_limit} and \code{decision_version}.

\subsection{Processing a Run in One Step}

For a detect-only run with fine powers, \code{plan-run} writes the plans, and \code{process-run} does the whole of steps 2 and 3:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy plan-run --bundle-dir bundle --run "/mnt/cs00/data/dtv/acq_*/dtv_fine_powers*.h5" \
  --epoch "2026-11 Pathfinder shakedown" --calibration-share 0.5 --output-dir run1/plans
pilot-proxy process-run --bundle-dir bundle --run "/mnt/cs00/data/dtv/acq_*/dtv_fine_powers*.h5" \
  --epoch "2026-11 Pathfinder commissioning" --calibration-share 0.5 --output-dir run1
\end{lstlisting}
\code{plan-run} sorts the files of each bound channel by the time of their first frame (\code{fpga_seq_time_nsec}), so a recording split by restarts keeps its order, and gives the first \code{--calibration-share} of them (default 2/3) to calibration and the rest to the test. It needs at least two files per channel. The bundle of the run must have a control channel, because the run then gives the control word. \code{process-run} needs a new or empty output directory and writes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{plans/calibrate.json} and \code{plans/test.json};
  \item \code{bundle/}, \code{bundle.report.json}, and \code{bundle.decisions.json} for a power-limit run;
  \item \code{test.json}, the \code{test-bundle} record;
  \item \code{spectrograms/calibrate/} and \code{spectrograms/test/}, with one \code{spectrogram_ch<NN>.pdf} and \code{.npz} per channel and \code{masked_summary.csv};
  \item \code{summary.md} and \code{summary.json}, with the masks at the default word: per channel the scale (v3) or the anchor (v1 and v2), the word and its source, the pilot share, the masked percent of each part, the longest masked stretch, and the test result.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Step 4: Deploy}
\label{sec:deploy}

A bundle from \code{calibrate-bundle} is detect-only. To mask with it, a person reads its test report, the report of Step~2 and the spectrograms, and then runs
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy qualify-bundle --bundle-dir bundle --test-report bundle.test.json \
  --reviewer "Name" --output-dir bundle-qualified
\end{lstlisting}
The command refuses a report that failed, that has no passing control check, that has no passing masked-fraction check of some calibrated channel, or that is of another bundle (\code{bundle_sha256sums}). It writes a new bundle in which every \code{calibrated} channel carries \code{mask_qualification}: the SHA-256 of the report and the reviewer. The new bundle has new digests, so it gets a new \code{<purpose>-<digest>} name.

Kotekan reads the bundle from a local path once, at startup. A bundle changes only at a restart. A passing bundle is added by a Kotekan pull request to \code{config/chime/dtv_bundles/} or \code{config/chord/dtv_bundles/}, under its \code{<purpose>-<digest>} name, with a line in that directory's \code{README.md}. After \code{make install} on the node, the digests are checked in place with \code{sha256sum -c sha256sums.txt}, and \code{dtv_runtime_bundle_dir} is set to the installed path. On the Pathfinder, the default of \code{dtv_runtime_bundle_dir} in \code{pathfinder.j2} is changed by a pull request, or the variable is set for one run.

\subsection{Startup and Binding}
\label{sec:startup}

At startup, each detector instance loads the bundle. It checks the schema, the namespace, the digests, the weight offsets against the compiled core, and the fine calibration of every \code{calibrated} or \code{control} profile. Every node loads the bundle, even one with no pilot. At the first frame, the instance reads the \code{coarse_freq} metadata of the voltage ring and binds each profile whose \code{receiver_channel_id} is among them. The binding stays fixed for the run. A change of \code{coarse_freq} after binding stops the run. A node with no bound profile runs with the detector disabled and writes zero masks, which mean the blocks were not tested.

\pfield{dtv_runtime_bundle_dir} & \pfield{data/pilotproxy_bundle} & The bundle directory, for example \pfield{/usr/local/etc/kotekan/chime/dtv_bundles/chime-first-56005bb7}.\\ \hline
\pfield{dtv_write_enabled} & false & Writes the products under \code{dtv_output_dir}. Without it the mask has no consumer and each frame is dropped.\\ \hline
\pfield{dtv_output_dir} & \code{/scratch/dtv} & Output directory.\\ \hline
\pfield{dtv_calibration_mode} & false & Adds \code{dtv_powers}, \code{dtv_fine_support} and \code{dtv_fine_powers}. The fine powers are HDF5, about 2.3 MB/s per NUMA domain.\\ \hline
\pfield{dtv_fine_powers_rows} & \code{num_freq_gpu_buf} & Rows of \code{dtv_fine_powers}. A node that binds more channels than this stops at its first frame.\\ \hline
\end{longtable}

With \code{dtv_write_enabled}, \code{rawFileWrite} writes \code{dtv_mask<sfx>}, with kotekan\#1924 \code{dtv_stat<sfx>} beside it (and, in calibration mode, \code{dtv_powers<sfx>} and \code{dtv_fine_support<sfx>}) as \code{.raw} files of 1024 frames, and \code{hdf5FileWrite} writes \code{dtv_fine_powers<sfx>} with 1024 frames per file. The detector runs and writes from startup. It is removed by a restart without \code{dtv_enabled}.

\subsection{CHORD F-engine Template}

\code{config/fengine/chord.j2} includes \code{include/dtv_chord.j2} when \code{dtv_enabled} or \code{dtv_apply_mask} is true. This template also takes \code{dtv_permanent_mask_freq_ids} (receiver IDs skipped by the detector and, with \code{dtv_apply_mask}, fully masked) and \code{dtv_require_fine_freq_ids} (receiver IDs that must have a fine test in every block). The two lists hold receiver IDs, must not overlap, and must name IDs on the node. The Kotekan user module describes them.

\subsection{Detect-only and Applying the Mask}
\label{sec:applymask}

In detect-only mode the detector records its decisions and the visibilities do not change. On the Pathfinder this is \code{/dtv_mask_enabled} off. On CHIME it is the only mode. With the mask on, \code{DtvRfiMask} clears every sample of a block at a frequency that the detector rejected, that has fine support \((0,n)\) (v1, v2), or that is in the permanent-mask list. For a v3 channel (kotekan\#1924) it reads \code{dtv_stat} and clears the block unless \code{pilotproxy_v3_keep} keeps it at the channel's BAO word, or its default word where there is none, and a block with \(d_\rho=0\) is cleared. The visibilities and the valid-sample counts change together.

Since Kotekan pull request \#1906, merged into \code{develop} through \#1914 on 20 November 2026, \code{DtvRfiMask} reads \code{pilot_profiles.json} at startup. Unless every \code{calibrated} channel carries \code{mask_qualification}, the bundle is detect-only: Kotekan refuses to start with the mask on, and a POST that switches \code{/dtv_mask_enabled} on stops Kotekan. \code{pending_campaign} channels never reach a mask, and the control reaches one only with \code{dtv_mask_control} (kotekan\#1915), so neither needs the record. No shipped bundle is qualified. A qualified bundle also goes through the shadow acceptance in \code{docs/OPERATOR_HANDOFF.md} before \code{dtv_apply_mask: true} is set.

\subsection{Bad Feeds}

On both telescopes the detector reads the bad feed mask ring of \code{bufferBadInputs} (\code{bf_mask_name: bf_mask}), and a flagged input is packed as zero. On CHORD, \code{bufferBadInputs} also flags the elements outside the main array, so the detector drops the 16 RFI antennas. On CHIME this was added by Kotekan pull request \#1880. \code{dtv_powers} and \code{dtv_fine_powers} fall as inputs are flagged. A word holds for the inputs summed at calibration. When the bundle records \code{summed_inputs} (from the plan's \code{num_inputs}, which \code{plan-run} does not write), the detector of Kotekan pull request \#1906 logs a warning while the count of good inputs differs from it by more than 10\%, and an INFO line when the count returns. The loop is repeated if the warning persists. On the Pathfinder the operators' script changes the mask as feeds misbehave: on 20 November 2026 it moved between 111 and 113 good inputs of 128 about every 14 minutes. The visibility files record it under \code{bad_feed_mask}. \code{tools/pathfinder_input_counts.py} reads it and prints each change, and \code{tools/calibration_length.py --input-counts} gives each channel's good inputs beside its word.

\section{Checking a Run}
\label{sec:checkrun}

\subsection{Log}

The first check is the log at the first frame. Each bound pilot has a line
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
PilotProxy: bound ATSC physical channel <ch> (pilot <MHz> MHz, receiver_channel_id <id>) to local frequency index <f>
\end{lstlisting}
which is checked against the frequency map of the node. A \code{reject-all limit} line marks a permanently masked ID. A \code{detector DISABLED} line means the node has no bundle pilot, and its zero masks mean the blocks were not tested. On the Pathfinder, \code{DTV mask on} or \code{off} logs a change of the mask switch.

\subsection{Products}

\begin{longtable}{|L{0.21\linewidth}|L{0.21\linewidth}|L{0.50\linewidth}|}
\caption{Detector products per local frequency and block.}\label{tab:products}\\
\hline
\textbf{Product} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Content}\\ \hline
\endfirsthead
\hline
\textbf{Product} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Content}\\ \hline

\code{dtv_mask} & int8 [1,F] & 1 rejects the block. Zero for unbound frequencies, for the control unless \code{dtv_mask_control} is set (kotekan\#1915), and for v3 channels, which \code{DtvRfiMask} decides from \code{dtv_stat}.\\ \hline
\code{dtv_stat} & uint64 [1,F,2] & v3 (kotekan\#1924): the pair \([n_\text{peak}, d_\rho]\). \([0, 2^{64}-1]\) for a row with no test, which every word keeps. A row with \(d_\rho = 0\) has no decision and is masked.\\ \hline
\code{dtv_fine_support} & int32 [1,F,2] & v1 and v2: \((1,n)\) valid test over \(n\) bulk bins; \((0,n)\) too few bulk bins, and its mask of 0 does not mean clean; \((-1,-1)\) no test (no pilot, permanent mask, detector disabled, the control without \code{dtv_mask_control}, or a v3 channel).\\ \hline

Under v1 and v2, a new line in the reference bins or a pilot that moves out of its designated set shows in \code{dtv_powers} and in the spectrograms before the mask changes, and either one calls for a recalibration. Under v3 the median ignores a line in the reference until half the even bins carry lines, and a new line anywhere in the channel raises \(n_{\mathrm{peak}}\), so it shows as a rise of the masked fraction.

\subsection{Spectrograms and Masked Percent}

A run with \code{dtv_calibration_mode} gives a spectrogram of each bound channel, with and without the mask:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy run-spectrogram --bundle-dir bundle \
  --run "/mnt/cs00/data/dtv/acq_*/dtv_fine_powers*.h5" \
  --channel 18 24 29 37 --output-dir spectrograms
\end{lstlisting}
The decision is recomputed from the fine powers and the bundle's default word (\code{cfar_multiplier_q16}), not the BAO word that \code{DtvRfiMask} applies where there is one, so the control shows what its word would remove. A block whose test is invalid counts as masked, as in \code{DtvRfiMask}. Chunks are 10 s by default (\code{--chunk-seconds}). A chunk with no kept blocks is grey. The time axis is recorded time, so a gap in the recording does not appear. The command also writes \code{masked_summary.csv} with the masked percent and the longest masked stretch of each channel. The percent on the control is its false-alarm rate at its word.

\subsection{Run Check Tools}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
python tools/check_pathfinder_dtv_run.py --dtv-dir /mnt/cs00/data/dtv --bundle-dir <bundle> \
  --vis-dir <visibility acq dir> --output-dir check
python tools/analyze_dtv_run.py --run-dir /scratch/dtv/<run> --bundle-dir <bundle>
\end{lstlisting}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{combine_pathfinder_dtv_run.py} stacks the per-window files of each closed acquisition into one file per half and product, \code{dtv_<product>.<half>.combined.h5}, and reads every window back against its source. A day of a calibration-mode run is about 56\,000 files. The run commands and \code{check_pathfinder_dtv_run.py} read either form.
  \item \code{check_pathfinder_dtv_run.py} reads the recv node's files. For each channel it reports windows and gaps, missing blocks, valid tests, the masked percent, the peak fine bin (against the bundle anchor under v1 and v2), and the visibilities' \code{frac_dtv_mask_enabled}.
  \item \code{analyze_dtv_run.py} reads the raw CHIME products. It needs all three of \code{dtv_mask}, \code{dtv_powers} and \code{dtv_fine_support}, so the run must have \code{dtv_calibration_mode}. It checks equal frame counts, gaps in \code{fpga_seq_num}, the zero rows of unbound frequencies, rank support (v1 and v2), and the masked fraction of each channel. With \code{--bundle-dir}, it decides the v3 channels from \code{dtv_stat} at each channel's word.
  \item \code{pilot-proxy replay-fine-capture} compares the stage with the reference bit for bit, from a baseband dump taken during the run (\code{docs/CAPTURE_FINE_REPLAY.md}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Score Cache}

To study the threshold of a channel by itself, \code{run-scores} writes each block's keeping word, its frame powers and its 10 s event:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy run-scores --bundle-dir bundle --run "/mnt/cs00/data/dtv/acq_*/dtv_fine_powers*.h5" \
  --channel 18 24 29 --jobs 32 --output-dir scores
python tools/threshold_rule.py --scores scores --power-limits limits.json --out thresholds
\end{lstlisting}
\code{run-scores} reads the block length and the sample period from each file. The result of \code{threshold_rule.py} is in-sample, so it is checked on a later part of the run or on another run.

\section{Standalone and Synthetic Use}

The standalone testbench checks the detector core on a generated ATSC signal. It needs a GPU, the CUDA library, and GNU Radio for the generator. The steps are:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy generate-atsc --gnuradio-python /usr/bin/python3 \
  --output-iq generated/atsc/atsc_8vsb_complex64.cfile --num-iq-samples 600000
pilot-proxy audit-atsc --input-iq generated/atsc/atsc_8vsb_complex64.cfile
pilot-proxy quantize --input-iq generated/atsc/atsc_8vsb_complex64.cfile \
  --physical-channel 14 --frame-size-samples 16384
pilot-proxy detect --input-detector-matrix generated/detector_input/detector_matrix_i4.npy
pilot-proxy evaluate-snr --input-iq generated/atsc/atsc_8vsb_complex64.cfile \
  --physical-channel 14 --frame-size-samples 16384 --num-input-streams 1 \
  --threshold-data-shelf-snr-db -26 --noise-trials 10 --noise-source python
\end{lstlisting}
\code{bash examples/quickstart.sh} runs the same chain. \code{generate-atsc} runs under the GNU Radio interpreter given by \code{--gnuradio-python}, which must be able to import PilotProxy from the checkout. \code{evaluate-snr --detector-backend cpu-reference} runs without a GPU. These commands use the shipped weight bank in \code{weights/} and the coarse frame ratio of the testbench, not a runtime bundle. Their options and outputs are described in \code{docs/USAGE.md}.

A development bundle for every channel is exported, with no calibration, by:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
pilot-proxy export-runtime-weight-bundle \
  --receiver-profile configs/receiver_profiles/chime_dtv_fengine.json \
  --weight-coordinate-system post_nyquist_zone_normalization \
  --physical-channel-range 14:36 --output-dir dev_bundle
\end{lstlisting}
Every profile of such a bundle is \code{pending_campaign}, so Kotekan stops when it binds one. The Kotekan integration tests copy such a bundle and fill it with synthetic thresholds.

The CHIME archive scan (\code{chime-survey}, \code{chime-scan}, \code{chime-combine}) reduces archived baseband data on CANFAR. It is described in \code{INTEGRATION.md} and \code{docs/CANFAR_RUNBOOK.md}.

\section{Known Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item No shipped bundle is qualified for masking. Every bundle made by \code{calibrate-bundle} is detect-only until a person runs \code{qualify-bundle} on it.
  \item A control word from simulated white noise is the lowest it can be. A real control can sit well above it, and a word from a short capture is a lower limit. The first CHIME bundle has such a word until a run with the control replaces it.
  \item \code{test-bundle} cannot show that a word catches weak pilots, since the captures do not say which blocks hold one.
  \item The detector masks only the coarse frequency that holds each pilot. A whole DTV channel is removed with the permanent-mask list of the F-engine template.
  \item Kotekan warns when the summed inputs differ from the calibration, and does not recalibrate. A bundle without \code{summed_inputs}, such as every shipped bundle or one from \code{plan-run}, is not checked.
  \item On CHIME the template has no mask path, so the detector is detect-only there.
  \item The decision covers only the DTV test, and under v1 and v2 the fine support reports only whether a block had enough bulk bins for the rank test. Packet loss and input health are handled by other Kotekan stages.
\end{itemize}
